# Learning-science survey and application (third review)
*2026-11-07 11:36 PST*

## Summary

1. The book's practice dose is now high (128 checks, 97 exercises), and 85 percent of the 415 callouts are practice containers rather than asides. The REFACTOR-PLAN worry about dose (Finding 28) no longer applies, and callouts are not overused.
2. Most serious: in the 600-page PDF, every one of the 128 answers prints directly under its question [V]. `conventions.qmd:56-59` tells readers that 'attempting the question is not optional'. That is true only in HTML.
3. The strongest evidence (retrieval spread out over time, spacing) is only partly used. Every check tests the section just read, with no delay. No exercise mixes problem types across chapters, and there are no faded or completion examples, although the readers are new to Docker and CI.
4. Accessibility work recorded as done is not done. The PDF is untagged, and the `scrbook` class cannot be tagged by Quarto. The `fig-gates` fix measured contrast against white, not between adjacent categories (1.34:1, hue only). The Simpson alt text still describes a pattern the data do not show. The stack figure used in 12 chapters encodes rung by hue alone.
5. The evidence for the conversational 'we' rewrite is moderate for short lessons and missing for 41- to 73-minute chapters. Its value here is in tone, not learning. The prose is still mostly third person ('the analyst', 'this chapter'), and further voice work should get low priority.

Evidence labels: [V] I ran it; [I] I read the source; [EK] expert knowledge I did not check; [U] source not opened. All web sources were accessed 2026-11-07. Several primary articles are behind paywalls. Where I relied on an abstract or a clearinghouse summary rather than the full paper, I say so.

What the evidence does not cover. I found no meta-analytic evidence on the best chapter length for a self-paced graduate text. Popular claims about a fixed 10-minute attention span have weak support [EK]. The personalization boundary at 35 minutes concerns the length of the study session, not the length of a chapter, so it is suggestive only.

## Part B. Application to this book

Structural basis: THIRD-REVIEW-PLAN.md lines 60-67 [V by the lead], plus my own scans in `work-ls/` (`scan.py`, `ctypes.py`, `check.R`, `contrast2.R`) [V]. File prefixes are not chapter numbers. Below, 'Ch' means the book's chapter number from `_quarto.yml`.

### Measured facts that change the picture

- **Callout composition [V].** 128 'Check your understanding', 128 nested 'Answer', 97 'Exercise N' solutions, 13 'The honest level', 12 'Before you start', 12 'The vocabulary of this chapter', 9 'Verification', 8 'Restricted data', 7 'Pitfall', 1 'Another warning about the words' (`02-levels-of-reproducibility.qmd:606`), and 1 habits box. So 353 of 416 callout blocks (85 percent) are practice containers. The true asides number about 5 per chapter. The figure '415 callouts' alone overstates the problem.
- **Visual coding [V].** Nine callout functions use four Quarto types. Checks, answers, solutions, 'Before you start', and the vocabulary box are all `callout-note`. 'Restricted data' and 'The honest level' are both `callout-important` (e.g. `04-dependencies.qmd:794`, `:1207`).
- **Figures [I].** 12 of 21 figures are the same determinant-stack diagram (one per chapter). The book has 9 distinct explanatory figures for about 134,000 words. Ch3, Ch8, Ch12, and Ch13 (02, 06, 09, 10) contain no figure besides the stack.
- **Voice [V].** First-person-plural forms (we, our, us) run at 2.1 to 6.6 per 1,000 words; 'you' and 'your' at 0 to 4.0 per 1,000. 'The analyst' and 'this chapter' remain the dominant subjects (e.g. `05-containers.qmd:200-230`). The average sentence is 17 to 22 words, with 8 to 22 sentences per chapter above 35 words.
- **Practice timing [I].** Each check closes the section it follows. An example is `04-dependencies.qmd:159-178`, placed straight after the Orientation anecdote at `:105-122`. No check asks about an earlier chapter's content, except where the topic overlaps. Exercises cite earlier chapters 0 to 3 times each (by `@sec-` or 'Chapter N' references). Some revisit an earlier chapter in prose without a cross-reference, e.g. `04-dependencies.qmd:1314-1324`.
- **Worked examples [I].** Each chapter has one '## Worked example', placed late: `04-dependencies.qmd:1064` of 1,620 lines and `09-continuous-integration.qmd:1391` of 1,992. A grep for completion or faded items (blanks, skeletons, 'fill in') found none. That is a structural check, not a test.

### Findings and recommendations, ranked

**1. Critical. In the PDF, every answer is printed under its question.**
`pdftotext` of `_book/Reproducible-Research-for-the-Health-Sciences.pdf` shows 'Check your understanding: naming the failure' followed straight away by 'Answer' and its text (PDF text lines 8304-8312, from `04-dependencies.qmd:159-178`). A second instance appears at lines 15044-15058 [V]. Quarto does not collapse callouts in LaTeX [V: observed]. `conventions.qmd:56-59` promises the opposite. REFACTOR-PLAN Finding 6 is marked RESOLVED, and it is resolved only for HTML. The retrieval attempt is the active ingredient (Yang 2021; Dunlosky 2013), so the 600-page edition, which students are likely to print or annotate, has no working retrieval practice.
*Fix.* For PDF output, move each answer out of its check: either to the end of the chapter, beside the Solutions, or to an 'Answers to checks' appendix with a back-link. Do this with a Lua filter or a `content-visible when-format='pdf'` pair, so the HTML keeps its nested fold. Change `conventions.qmd:56-59` so it describes both editions accurately.

**2. Major. Retrieval practice is immediate and never spaced or mixed across chapters.**
All 128 checks test material from the preceding few hundred words. This mostly tests reading comprehension, and it adds nothing that spacing would add. Cepeda et al. (2006) show that the useful gap grows with the retention interval. For a quarter-long course with a final project, gaps of days to weeks matter. Interleaving pays off most when categories are similar (Brunmair & Richter 2019). The book's central skill, diagnosing which determinant layer failed (packages, OS, RNG, stale target, data), is exactly such a set of confusable categories.
*Fix.* (a) Open each chapter's 'Before you start' card (e.g. `04-dependencies.qmd:36-79`) with two or three folded retrieval questions on earlier chapters. This also delivers the 'skip-ahead quiz' of the 10.7 template, which was never implemented [I: no such quiz found in `04`/`05`]. (b) Replace one end-of-section check per chapter with a cumulative item. (c) Add a mixed diagnosis set of 10 to 15 short failure vignettes, each requiring the reader to name the layer and the tool, to `09-continuous-integration.qmd` (Ch12, after 'Debugging a failed reproduction', `:1464`) and to the instructor guide for a week-9 quiz. (d) Have `instructor-guide.qmd` schedule low-stakes cumulative quizzes. At present it has no mention of retrieval or spacing [I: grep].

**3. Major. There is no fading from worked example to independent practice for the unfamiliar tools.**
The audience is expert in R and novice in Docker, CI YAML, Make, and `targets`. The expertise-reversal literature (MIT summary of Kalyuga & Renkl 2010) predicts that full worked examples help on the new tools and add little on R material the readers already know. The book does the reverse of fading: one late worked example, then full open problems (e.g. `04-dependencies.qmd:1270-1330`).
*Fix.* In Ch7 (containers, `05-containers.qmd:1216`), Ch10 (pipelines), and Ch12 (CI), add a three-step sequence: a complete Dockerfile, `_targets.R`, or workflow; the same artifact with two or three lines blanked out and a test that fails until they are filled; then a free exercise. Move each chapter's worked example nearer the chapter's start, where it can anchor the exposition. Do not add worked examples for plain R material.

**4. Major. Accessibility defects recorded as fixed remain.**
(a) The PDF reports `Tagged: no` [V, pdfinfo], and the book uses `documentclass: scrbook` (`_quarto.yml:97`). Per Quarto's documentation, KOMA classes cannot yet produce correctly tagged PDFs. A tagged edition therefore needs `book` plus `pdf-standard: ua-2`, not only the option. Changing the class changes layout, which is a decision for the lead.
(b) `fig-gates` (`03-research-compendium.qmd:972-982`). The 2026-10-15 fix measured each fill against white (REFACTOR-PLAN line 2779). 'Universal' (`#1a5c8c`) against 'Selective' (`#1e7a50`) is 1.34:1, and both carry the identical white circle, so those two categories differ by hue alone [V]. That fails WCAG 1.4.1. Use different glyphs (for example a filled and a hollow circle) or text labels in the cells.
(c) The Simpson alt text (`07-literate-programming.qmd:453`, Ch9) still says 'the species clusters sit at descending heights as bill length increases'. The generated cluster means are (38.7 mm, 18.47), (47.2, 15.05), and (49.2, 18.61): two clusters at the same height and one lower one between them [V, re-ran the chunk's generator]. The slope clause, pooled -0.061, is now correct. Rewrite the position clause.
(d) The determinant-stack palette (`R/stack_diagram.R:96-100`) encodes rung by fill only. Eight of ten color pairs fall below 3:1 (L0 against 'L2 partially pinned' is 1.05:1) [V]. The generated alt text covers screen readers, but sighted color-deficient readers get only the legend. Print the rung name on each bar.
(e) No `axe` check is configured in `_quarto.yml` [I]. Add `axe: {output: document}` under a debug profile. Note that it runs only in preview.

**5. Moderate. Callout styling is not discriminable, and the card duplicates the roadmap.**
Signaling helps when the cue tells the reader what kind of content follows (Schneider et al. 2018). Five functions sharing `callout-note`, and the 'Restricted data' rule sharing a style with the 'honest level' summary, weaken exactly the safety cue the book most needs noticed. Separately, the card's **Outline** (`04-dependencies.qmd:68-79`) and the Orientation's roadmap paragraph (`:147-157`) list the same sequence twice within one screen. This is print-on-print redundancy. The evidence against it is weak [EK], but it costs words in a book already about 50 percent over the quarter's load (THIRD-REVIEW-PLAN line 57).
*Fix.* Give 'Restricted data' its own class (`callout-caution` with a distinct icon) and keep it unique. Delete either the card's Outline or the roadmap paragraph in all 12 chapters, since the HTML sidebar TOC already carries the outline. Retire or fold the one-off 'Another warning about the words' (`02:606`). REFACTOR-PLAN 10.7 asked for three callout kinds. Nine exist, and the six extra kinds are defensible, but they should be listed in `conventions.qmd`.

**6. Moderate. There are too few distinct explanatory figures, and the most complex topics have none.**
The contiguity effect is largest for static 2-D diagrams and adult learners (g of about 0.7, Schroeder & Cenkci). Ch12 (CI, 11,840 words) has no figure but the stack. The section 'What the checks are: the three gates' runs from `09-continuous-integration.qmd:374` to `:637` with one subheading. A diagram of trigger, runner, three gates, and verdict, placed beside the YAML, would carry what that prose now carries. Ch13 (archiving) needs a deposit and access-tier diagram, and Ch3 (levels) needs a ladder diagram, unless the stack is meant to serve as one. Repeating the stack 12 times is a sound orienting device, so keep it, but it should not count toward the visual budget.
*Fix.* Add one purpose-drawn diagram each to Ch12, Ch13, and Ch8, with labels placed in the diagram rather than in a legend.

**7. Moderate. The running example is present but not yet a whole game.**
Cohort mentions now run from 10 to 29 per chapter [V], and each chapter has a 'cohort' section (e.g. `04-dependencies.qmd:1139`). The REFACTOR-PLAN Finding 11 and 17 gaps are largely closed. Opening anecdotes still use other scenarios (the registry student, `04-dependencies.qmd:105-122`), against house rule 5 (REFACTOR-PLAN 10.6). The *R Packages* device, one short executed end-to-end pass early that later chapters deepen, is still absent.
*Fix.* Use the cohort in the openings where it fits. Consider a short 'whole game' run of the cohort compendium in Ch1 or Ch5, ending at L1, with each step linked forward.

**8. Low. The 'we' voice: keep it, and stop investing in it.**
Ginns et al. (2013) find benefits of conversational style for short lessons and none reliable past 35 minutes. Their manipulations are second-person address, not 'we'. The book's chapters take 41 to 73 minutes, and the voice pass left the prose mostly third person (2 to 7 'we' forms per 1,000 words). No learning benefit should be claimed for the rewrite. It is harmless and suits the field's register. The measurable gains are in Findings 1 to 3. Breaking up the 8 to 22 sentences per chapter above 35 words would help readability more than further pronoun changes [EK].

**9. Low. Epigraphs and 'honest level' boxes as possible seductive details.**
Thirteen epigraphs precede the cards. The seductive-details penalty was larger on paper and when the irrelevant item sat next to relevant material (WSU summary). The epigraphs are thematically relevant, so the risk is small [EK]. The 'honest level' box (13 instances) earns its place only if it says something specific to the chapter. Earlier reviews found it reciting the ladder, and I did not re-check all 13.

### Where this differs from REFACTOR-PLAN section 10

- **Finding 28 ('the dose of practice, not its design, is the issue').** The dose has since been fixed, and the design is now the issue: answers show in the PDF, checks are immediate, there is no cumulative or mixed retrieval, and there is no fading.
- **Finding 6 (RESOLVED).** It is resolved only for HTML; see Finding 1 above.
- **Finding 28 on voice ('evidence for a conversational register is moderate').** I agree for short lessons. For this book's chapter lengths the evidence is weak, because of the session-length boundary condition, and the studies do not test 'we'.
- **Finding 27 and the 2026-10-15 fix.** The `fig-gates` contrast was measured against the wrong reference. The Simpson alt-text fix covered the slope but not the cluster positions.
- **Template 10.7.** The 'three callout kinds only' rule and the 'skip-ahead quiz' were not implemented. The first deviation is defensible; the second is worth doing, as cumulative retrieval rather than a pretest.
- **Agreement.** Callouts are not overused once practice containers are separated out. The one-check-per-section rule was implemented as planned.

## Scope and limitations

- I did not render the book. The PDF observations come from the lead's 2026-11-07 build in `_book/`.
- Several primary meta-analyses (Barbieri 2023, Kalyuga 2007, Schroeder & Cenkci 2018, Schneider 2018, Sundararajan & Adesope 2020) were read through abstracts or clearinghouse and university summaries, not the full text. PubMed pages failed to load (cookie wall), so I used Europe PMC's API for the Cepeda, Brunmair, and Dunlosky abstracts.
- The voice and sentence metrics come from regular expressions over the `.qmd` source with code chunks removed. They are approximate.
- I did not check screen-reader behavior, HTML heading order, or code-block accessibility. I did not run `axe`.
- I did not assess factual items from `factual-issues.md`. None falls in this brief's domain [I: grep for alt, color, accessibility, callout].
